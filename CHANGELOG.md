## 0.30.0 — Spider and cave spider surface movement, one bounded short leap, and native poison

**Build:** Minecraft 1.21.1 / NeoForge 21.1.216; `mod_version` 0.30.0, artifact `tactical-neoforge-1.21.1-0.30.0.jar`. Protocols unchanged (Tactical **12**, Skill **4**, BattleLog **2**). Complete with limited real-client acceptance; not a public release.

- **Two exact types, registered separately.** `minecraft:spider` and `minecraft:cave_spider` each get the surface adapter, matched by exact `EntityType` only. No superclass or other mob inherits this displacement, nothing is marked Flying, and a ridden body never enters it.
- **It climbs a real wall face.** `SpiderSurface` nodes keep the foot cell, the real feet, the surface kind and the wall side; the wall-hugging position comes from the body's real half-width, so a 1.4-wide body never has its centre pressed into a neighbouring cell.
- **Whole body, whole edge, every chunk proved first.** Nodes and every swept leg prove the involved chunks are loaded before any block, liquid or collision read, and the body box must be inside the world and the battlefield with no liquid, hazard, roster occupant, bystander or foreign body.
- **Bounded search.** At most 512 nodes per own turn, depth limited by the current AP after reserving one melee; each edge is priced by the existing `state.moveCost` and shares the existing prepayment and unwalked-edge refund without adding a paid action.
- **The native climbing bit no longer overrides the controlled pose.** Two narrow mixins: `Spider#tick` restates the climbing bit for this body and `LivingEntity#travel` skips gravity integration for an exact controlled spider really on a wall. Ticks, `aiStep`, fire, effects, freezing and damage are untouched, and spiders outside the encounter are unchanged.
- **No wall means no wall.** Support is re-proved every tick; when it fails the stale pose is cleared before the body is handed to the existing `terrainFall`. An illegal edge stops the body at its real position, refunds exactly the unwalked edges and leaves no permanent busy; a normal exit keeps the real end position.
- **Real-body melee gate.** Plan, re-proof and execution share the native attack box at the real feet (horizontal expansion only), the four-way adjacency and the real eye-line LOS. A 0.9-tall spider no longer hits one level up on a grid `dy=1`, while fractional feet still decide by real intersection. `doHurtTarget` stays the only damage entry and only a real hit pays the existing melee price.
- **One bounded short leap, not invented pounce damage.** New `SpiderLeap`: ground take-off and real ground landing, locked opponent at a real 3D distance of 2 to 4 blocks (the native `LeapAtTargetGoal` band) that melee cannot reach yet, landing cell within Manhattan 2, 2.5 blocks horizontally and 1 vertically. At most 16 real landings are examined and cached once per own turn plus epoch; later re-proofs re-check that same immutable proposal. Each slice sweeps the whole body against chunk loading, blocks, liquid, hazards, other bodies and the battlefield; the roster gate reads the cells the real foot point passes through.
- **A leap is chosen only when cheaper; commit and first frame are two transactions.** Melee first; otherwise one bounded route is planned and the leap is used only when that route cannot reach or costs at least three edges. One successful leap per own turn; `ap.spider.leapCost` (base **10 AP**) follows the shared surcharge and is frozen at commit. Only the first real displacement subtracts exactly that frozen price and adds one paid action; an unaffordable pool refuses instead of being clamped. Zero displacement and any change before lift-off are re-proved at the first frame against the same cached proposal and refused free, without a paid action or the quota.
- **An interrupted spider never hands its turn over before the fall is its own.** All three stop paths establish the fall barrier before publishing state and handing the turn on, so no tick leaves commands live or lets the other side recover AP while the body is still in the air.
- **Broken mid-arc stops at the real position.** Nothing already flown is refunded, the body stops where it really is, the pose is cleared and the existing fall rule takes over; no pull-back, no restored start, no permanent busy. After landing, target, real attack box, adjacency, real eye LOS and AP are re-proved for at most one native `doHurtTarget` (10 then 13 AP by default); otherwise the turn is handed over with no further walk or leap.
- **The cave spider's poison is still the native poison.** Its only source is that spider's successful `doHurtTarget`: NORMAL **140** ticks, HARD **300** ticks, EASY none, amplifier 0; cancelling the native damage adds no poison and charges nothing, and both spider types stay immune. A newly added poison only baselines at the target's next own start, the wall clock never advances it, and a later complete own cycle applies its pulse and expires without killing; per-unit release never deletes a real instance. That is not by itself a cross-encounter isolation proof.

## 0.29.0 — Amphibious drowned and bounded enderman tactics

**Build:** Minecraft 1.21.1 / NeoForge 21.1.216; `tactical-neoforge-1.21.1-0.29.0.jar`. Protocols remain Tactical **12**, Skill **4**, BattleLog **2**. This source update is not a public binary release.

- **Drowned use their real equipment.** Only a main-hand trident enables the new throw, with the hand checked again at execution. Vanilla creates the real `ThrownTrident`: its own origin, nominal 1.6 launch speed, spread, damage, sound, gravity and 0.99 air/water inertia are retained. The held stack, NBT and durability are unchanged; nothing is equipped just because of an entity type.
- **Water and shore are both reachable.** An exact-type amphibious policy supports water admission, fractional water positions, bounded 3D swimming and shore transitions. Real swept-body paths, per-segment checks, occupancy and loaded-chunk gates prevent clipping or free flight. Waiting units do not drift; interrupted movement returns the unused movement cost.
- **One throw, one transaction.** The base price is 20 AP (`ap.drowned.tridentCost`) plus the existing surcharge, once per own turn. A real projectile must enter the world before charging; cancelled or refused creation is free, while a shot already fired is not refunded for missing. Battle tridents cannot be picked up and are reclaimed on settlement, timeout, owner exit or encounter end. Their collision guard survives cancelled impacts and protects same-side units, bystanders, non-living bodies and other encounters. Wild and player tridents are unchanged.
- **Endermen use vanilla teleportation, not a simulated jump.** The native private entry retains the cancellable/re-pointable `EnderEntity` event, portal particles and sound. Before movement, the event's final destination is checked against the real origin: at most 8 blocks, not a no-op, loaded chunks, complete solid footing, full body clearance, no liquid/hazard/occupant, and the whole body inside the encounter. A water escape must actually lead to a dry, sheltered point.
- **Active relocation has a clear budget.** One cached proposal examines at most 32 cells per own turn. A successful teleport costs a base 20 AP (`ap.enderman.teleportCost`) and one paid action, updates the real cell/occupancy, then allows at most one legal melee follow-up at its raised price (13 AP by default). Failure is free; there is no repeated teleport or movement follow-up loop.
- **Reactions are limited and source-scoped.** Only a same-encounter opponent's native projectile/potion hit can trigger one successful reaction per cycle, using at most 16 candidate cells and the fixed base teleport price. It does not advance the queue, epoch or paid-action count. Cancelled attempts retain the success quota. Ordinary melee does not dodge; unaffordable or refused dodges retain the native hurt result and projectile immunity. Bystanders cannot schedule a later paid escape.
- **Water sensitivity follows own turns.** The controlled enderman's wall-clock water/rain pulse is deferred to one native 1.0 drown call at its own turn start, followed by a dry walk or paid escape. With no dry point it stays instead of buying a useless move. Water bottles and other damage paths retain vanilla behaviour. Exit and admission rollback clear owned temporary state without replacing carried blocks or equipment. No new UI, dependency or network fields were introduced.

**Verified on the frozen source:** 94 common and 818 NeoForge Java tests (**912** total, zero failures/errors/skips), 51 release-script tests, packaging checks, the new drowned/enderman dedicated-server fixtures, and scoped regressions for ranged mobs, blaze, witch, flight, effects, teams, companions, multiple battles and block terrain. Coverage includes fractional shore movement, collision isolation, projectile cleanup, native teleport veto/re-points, bounded reactions, raised-price melee and own-turn water damage.

**Real-client evidence (limited):** real End/facing input, native trident flight and damage, water admission/holding and an actual walk ashore; a clean native teleport followed by the 13 AP melee; real bow input triggering one paid reaction without changing the turn; and an old-position click control. Positive world selection is retained from the unchanged picking path of the earlier reaction scene, with its source recorded separately. A final controlled-water scene records the native own-turn 1.0 hit followed by a real walk onto dry stone and wet state clearing; the new Chinese teleport wording is visible. The entire longest Chinese water-clock sentence was not visually certified in the small log viewport. These runs use development source-set outputs with Dungeons Perspective 3.6.3, not an independently launched packaged JAR. Broader aquatic AI, enderman block ecology, live Shulker teleport and a full rain matrix are outside this finite acceptance.

## 0.28.0 — A blaze that charges, fires its own three native fireballs, and keeps them inside the encounter

**Build**: `0.28.0`, Minecraft 1.21.1 / NeoForge 21.1.216; artifact `tactical-neoforge-1.21.1-0.28.0.jar`. Protocols unchanged from 0.27.0: Tactical **12**, Skill **4**, BattleLog **2**. The blaze stage and its scoped automated, server, regression and real-client checks are complete; this does not authorize a public release.

- **One more monster type is recognised, by exact entity id.** `minecraft:blaze` joins the registry next to creeper, skeleton, stray, bogged, pillager, witch, vindicator and phantom. No superclass is matched, so no other monster inherits the fireball adapter or the air movement.
- **The fireball is the real vanilla fireball, built the way vanilla builds it.** A blaze is not a `RangedAttackMob` and has no callable ranged method, so the salvo creates a genuine `SmallFireball` with the entity as its owner, the vanilla spawn point (`getY(0.5) + 0.5`), the vanilla `Mth.triangle` spread on the two horizontal axes only, the vanilla `normalize()` launch, and therefore the vanilla 0.1 initial speed, +0.1 per-tick acceleration, 0.95 inertia, no gravity, 5.0 direct fire damage, post-attack enchantment effects and five seconds of ignition. Nothing is faked, no bow is equipped and no `performRangedAttack` is invented.
- **The vanilla charge is the entity's own bit.** A minimal mixin invoker exposes `Blaze#setCharged`/`isCharged`, so the presentation is the game's `isOnFire()` and not a second flag. The bit is cleared on every exit: normal hand-back, a lost target, a refused salvo, death, removal, an abandoned fall and encounter end.
- **One own turn is one bounded salvo.** 60 ticks of charge, then up to three fireballs six ticks apart, then the turn goes back only after every projectile has settled - a hard ceiling of 160 server ticks. Vanilla's own 100-tick wall-clock cooldown becomes "once per own turn": this is a turn-based adaptation and is stated as such, not simulated.
- **Exactly one paid action, committed by reality.** The price is frozen before anything happens (base 20 AP, configurable as `ap.blazeVolleyCost`, plus the shared +25% per prior paid action of the same turn). Nothing is charged until a fireball really joined the world; a salvo whose joins were all cancelled or refused charges nothing and leaves no residue; a salvo whose first fireball exists and whose later shot fails pays once, refunds nothing and never retries.
- **Only this encounter's marked fireballs obey this encounter's rule.** The collision entry refuses a same-side unit, the shooter itself, a bystander, another encounter's unit and any non-living body - and it refuses a block hit too - by cancelling and removing the projectile before vanilla's hit code runs. The legal target keeps the untouched vanilla path. A blaze in the wild and every other projectile are unchanged.
- **A controlled blaze never sets the world on fire,** because the projectile never reaches `onHitBlock`: `EntityMobGriefingEvent` is also denied for a controlled blaze, while the gamerule itself, other mobs and fire that already existed are left alone.
- **The blaze carries itself through the encounter's existing air movement.** Exact `minecraft:blaze` support in `AerialMovement` reuses the whole-body clearance, swept collision, 26-neighbour search budget, real movement cost and fractional altitude offset. It keeps its last real position on release; nothing teleports it back or drops it to the ground.
- **Melee needs real reach, not grid adjacency.** In vanilla's own `distanceToSqr < 4.0` band with a clear sight line the blaze uses the native `doHurtTarget`; otherwise it fires from where it stands, and only if neither is legal does it move - within the AP it really has - to a cell it can really shoot from. Shooting is capped at the ruleset's 16 blocks and further clamped by the entity's real follow range; the comfortable band is 6-12 blocks. An under-priced actor keeps its resources and ends its turn instead of walking forever.

## 0.27.0 — Ranged monsters that really fire what they carry, a witch who drinks and throws on her own turn, and a vindicator who takes the door apart

**Build**: `0.27.0`, Minecraft 1.21.1 / NeoForge 21.1.216; artifact `tactical-neoforge-1.21.1-0.27.0.jar`. Protocols unchanged from 0.26.0: Tactical **12**, Skill **4**, BattleLog **2**. All three approved stages and their scoped automated, server and single-client acceptance checks are complete; this does not authorize a public release.

- **Three more monster types are recognised by entity id.** `minecraft:pillager`, `minecraft:stray` and `minecraft:bogged` are now registered exactly, next to the existing creeper, skeleton and phantom. Everything else still falls back to the original generic melee adapter, so no other mob changes behaviour.
- **The weapon decides the ranged action, not the mob type.** A held bow and a held crossbow are detected in the main or off hand with the same hand rule vanilla uses, so planning, the quoted price and the hand that really fires are one decision. The quote is the bow's 15 AP or the crossbow's 20 AP from the shared ruleset, and an actor holding neither keeps its original melee: nothing is equipped by entity type.
- **A crossbow reloads and fires inside a single own turn.** An empty crossbow is loaded through the vanilla item path (`releaseUsing` at full charge), which draws the monster's own `getProjectile` ammunition - never a player inventory - and then fires in the same turn for one paid action. A crossbow that is already charged uses its real `ChargedProjectiles` instead of being reloaded.
- **The salvo is one action, the arrows are all real.** Multishot, piercing and durability are the vanilla ones, the whole salute costs one 20 AP action once, the turn stays with the shooter until every projectile has settled, and a later miss, block or cancelled hit refunds nothing.
- **Unsupported ammunition is refused, not faked.** A crossbow loaded with a firework rocket is declined with a recorded reason and keeps its real load and its AP; the crossbow arrow profile is 3.15 speed, so an arrow is never fired on bow numbers and rocket ballistics are not pretended.
- **Tipped arrows stay vanilla.** Stray and bogged reuse the bow adapter and nothing else: the real entity's own `performRangedAttack -> getArrow` produces the arrow, so slowness 600 ticks and poison 100 ticks arrive with their native values and then age on the affected participant's own-turn effect clock.
- **One opponent per own turn.** The turn locks the opponent's UUID; execution re-validates that same entity instead of looking up the nearest one again, and a lost target allows exactly one bounded replan before the turn ends.
- **Bilingual wording** for the two new journal structures (lost-target replan, unsupported rocket) in `zh_cn` and `en_us`.
- **A witch drinks for herself on her own turn only.** Vanilla picks and counts down a potion inside `Witch#aiStep`, outside the goal system, so no-AI alone was never enough to stop her from healing on somebody else's turn. While an encounter controls her, that one server-side segment is skipped with a targeted injection (her own `super.aiStep` and every other entity hook still run), and a potion she was already drinking when she joined is taken out of the entity: it cannot settle on wall-clock time, and it has to be paid for on her next own turn or be given back untouched when she leaves. A self-drink is a real, bounded presentation rather than a single instant: the borrowed hand gets the bottle, vanilla's own drinking flag, countdown and slowdown are used, the turn stays busy for that window and exactly one settlement at the end applies the dose, charges the action and gives the borrowed item back. Only a drink that really happened is paid for - an instant heal goes through vanilla's own instant outlet and a refused or cancelled one costs nothing and starts no cooldown - and merely holding a bottle or an axe is never treated as a drink.
- **One self-drink per own turn, on one shared 3-own-turn cooldown.** Every self-brew draws from the same cooldown, so she cannot dodge it by switching to swiftness, and the cooldown is counted in her own turns: with a gap of 3 she may drink on her 1st own turn, not on the 2nd or 3rd, and again on the 4th. Wall-clock time, other units acting and a reopened menu never advance or reset it, and two witches never share one.
- **Deterministic self-drink order.** Emergency healing at 25% health first, then water breathing while her eyes are in water, fire resistance while she burns, then ordinary healing below the configured threshold (default 50%) and only when at least one whole dose would land, then swiftness past 11 blocks, otherwise nothing. A drink that would be wasted or duplicated is not taken, and a heal never writes health directly: the real potion effect goes through the existing own-turn effect clock.
- **One thrown bottle per own turn at the opponent she locked.** Slowness at 8+ blocks without one, poison against a target that can still lose 8 health, weakness in the target's face, otherwise instant harming - and never harming against a target it would heal. Planning reserves the bottle's own 15 AP and works for a legal throwing position inside 10 horizontal blocks, preferring 4-8, so she steps back out of reach, closes in, or moves sideways instead of throwing through cover.
- **The bottle is a real vanilla splash potion.** She creates a real `ThrownPotion` carrying the registry potion her decision named, thrown with a potion's own flight (0.75 speed, 0.05 gravity, 0.99 air / 0.8 water drag) rather than a bow's or a crossbow's, with vanilla's own splash application, distance falloff and level event. Creating the bottle is the paid event: a bottle that never joins the world costs nothing and leaves nothing behind, while a bottle that exists is final even if it misses.
- **Its splash only reaches this encounter's opposed participants.** The bottle is marked when it is created and the candidate list is narrowed once, before vanilla's per-entity loop, so one rule covers instant damage and every periodic effect alike: the throwing witch herself, a same-side raider, a bystander and any other encounter's unit are untouched, while ordinary potions outside a battle and a player's own potions keep vanilla behaviour.
- **Two new journal structures** for a paid drink and a thrown bottle, plus a no-throw refusal, with the brew name carried as structure, in `zh_cn` and `en_us`. Four new settings (`witch.drinkCost`, `witch.throwCost`, `witch.drinkCooldownTurns`, `witch.healThreshold`) default to the ruled 25 / 15 / 3 / 0.5.

- **A vindicator keeps the vanilla axe and gains only a route.** `minecraft:vindicator` is registered exactly, reusing the native melee outlet: the real iron axe, its real item attributes, its enchantments and vanilla's own `doHurtTarget`, sound, swing and knockback are what hurt the target, and there is no second hit and no hard-coded damage. It picks its opponent from the same approved pool as every other hostile (this battle's players and controlled companions) - a `Johnny` name does **not** widen that pool to villagers, golems or animals.
- **A door is the last resort, not the first idea.** Each own turn the vindicator asks two separate questions of the same authoritative terrain: is there a complete route that needs no door broken, and - only if that answer is a proven "no" - is there a complete route if exactly the closed wooden doors on it were gone. A detour is always walked first, even when this turn's AP cannot finish it. The search reports FOUND / UNREACHABLE / LIMIT_REACHED separately, so a budget or loaded-area limit is never read as "the only way is a door".
- **Walking through an open doorway is real collision, not a special case.** The battle's plain terrain adapter refuses any non-empty collision shape, which also refuses the thin leaf of an already open door. A door-capable actor therefore asks a door-aware view instead: the real body box at the grid centre and the swept body of the whole step decide whether a leaf is in the way, and only that actor's movement validation and movement recheck use it. No other creature gains a door adjacency, and no path is planned through a door that is still closed.
- **One closed wooden door pair, one break, one price.** Only blocks in `minecraft:wooden_doors` that are really door halves are candidates; the lower and upper half are one object. The turn may pay the real steps first and then break at the door's front for the shared `breakCost` (default 10), the break ends that own turn with the remaining AP kept, and the next own turn recomputes the route - so nobody gets a free axe swing through the hole in the same turn. The removal drops exactly one door, plays the real break particles and publishes the block-destroy event, and both halves disappear together.
- **Every refusal is free.** Before the world changes, the turn re-checks the same pair (still there, still closed, still wooden, loaded, inside the zone), the actor's standing in front of it, the real vanilla protection chain - `CommonHooks.canEntityDestroy`, i.e. the loaded chunk, the entity-griefing event, the block's own rule and the cancellable `LivingDestroyBlockEvent` - the events not having swapped the door out, and that nothing standing on the door would lose its footing. A door that was opened, removed or replaced meanwhile, an event refusal, a difficulty below normal or mob griefing switched off all change nothing and charge nothing. No player identity is borrowed and the player-only break event is not used as entity protection.
- **Three new journal structures** for a paid door break, a refused break and a route that changed under a walking actor, in `zh_cn` and `en_us`. No new settings: the break reuses `breakCost`.
